Why these wings, and why this price?

We do not put every student under the same wing model. Miki usually sees within your first day or two on the training hill what kind of pilot you are becoming, and matches the wing to you, not the other way round. For most students, roughly 8 in 10 in our experience, the right choice is what we call a “goto” wing: the sweet spot of the EN-A class, where very good passive safety meets light, honest handling and real performance; these are wings on which our students fly their first cross-country triangles, and on which a skilled pilot can fly their first 100 km. That is what the CHF 5,500 (Regular) or CHF 6,000 (Light) set price buys. If you are more cautious, or simply want the calmest possible ride with no ambition beyond enjoying the view, we can put you under a low-A wing instead, a bit more of a “floating armchair,” slower to turn but with the highest passive safety in the class, typically CHF 400 to 500 less than the goto price. And if you turn out to be a fast, confident learner, we may suggest stepping up to a high-A wing later: still full EN-A passive safety, but meaningfully more performance, typically around CHF 1,000 more.

A package price built on the simplest, slowest EN-A on the market looks better on paper; we spec your set for your second and third year in the sport, not just for the training hill. The price also includes what a webshop cannot ship: the wing and harness are chosen and sized for your exact body weight, the manufacturer warranty runs through us, and the person who sold it to you is the same person who sees it at every check and every training day afterwards. And when you outgrow it, we take it in trade against your next wing.

Bringing your own equipment?

Then the course is CHF 6,000. Our course price is low because it is partly funded by the equipment package, and we prefer to tell you that openly. That way we earn about the same whichever you choose, so our advice on your gear can stay honest. The course with your own kit includes a 30-minute review of your equipment: I look at your wing, harness and reserve and tell you honestly whether I consider them airworthy, because I will not send you into the air on gear I have not seen. If the review shows something needs a closer look, I can do a full check and a reserve repack for you at our Checkcenter (before the course starts), or recommend the right equipment if the honest conclusion is that yours should not fly. My advice either way: talk to me before you buy anything, anywhere. Porous fabric, lines shrunk out of trim, a reserve unpacked for years, a wing outside your weight range: none of it is visible in a photo, and a check costs a fraction of a wrong purchase. Buying used somewhere else? As a student of the course, send me the listing first, for free.

Transparency, and no fine print

Why the total, and not just our course price?

Because CHF 10,550 to 11,550 is what you will actually spend. You will find schools advertising CHF 2,000 or 3,000; those numbers are real, but they are fragments: a part-course without the training hill, or a price that only applies if you buy your wing there. Wherever you end up in Switzerland, the honest end total lands in the same range. We simply make it transparent, so you can choose your instructor instead of decoding a price list.

And why are we not the cheapest?

Because we refuse the two standard ways of getting cheaper: bigger groups and lower-quality gear. A school that puts several times as many students on one instructor can keep its costs lower. A package built on an older or lower-quality wing looks better on paper. We teach one intensive course a year with max. 10 students, and we spec your equipment for your second and third year in the sport, not just for the exam. What you get for the difference shows up where it matters: on a normal training day our students typically log 4 to 6 flights, where 2 to 3 is the norm in large groups, each one with an instructor actually watching. Count it per flight, and per minute of real attention, and the difference often turns around. The Ultimate Guide to Choosing Your School →

At that price you are buying one of the most affordable entries into real aviation. Your wing lasts for years, the thermals are free, and once you hold the licence a full day of flying costs you a cable-car ticket.
