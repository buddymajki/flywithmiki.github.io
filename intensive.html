Why one summer, and why intensive weeks?

A course spread over a whole year has a known weakness: every gap breaks your rhythm, and the weather and your calendar rarely line up. One summer with fixed days keeps the momentum, and the whole group flies together, on the same days. The three intensive weeks in June are where the fastest progress happens: flying every day while everything is still fresh. The weekends then give everyone, intensive or not, the room to reach the 55 to 80 flights most students need for the exam. A rainy weekend is one of eight, not a third of your course.

And we teach beginners only in summer, on purpose. From June to August the school flies; from September to February it is closed for teaching, and that time goes into the office work and the instructor's rest. That is exactly why on your flying days you get all of him, rested. A rested instructor is a safer instructor.

Why summer? The longest days, the most flyable weekends, warm launches: from June to August you fly more per weekend in Central Switzerland than in any other season. That is how you progress fastest with the fewest days off work. Strong midday thermals are not for beginners, so we fly you in the calm morning and evening air; the long days make room for both.

Away for two or three weeks in summer? Most people are. Tell us early, and we plan your progress around it on the call.

How It Works

1
From January: theory at your own pace
Theory self-study with the FlightDeck app (approx. 1 hour a day for 2 to 3 weeks, whenever it suits you), SHV membership and insurance set up with our help.
2
By mid-April: your set
We choose and fit your set together at an appointment in Kriens, and order it by mid-April. Bringing your own kit? It gets an airworthiness check before the course, so nothing unknown flies with us. Want to try paragliding before you book? A Trial Day on the training hill (Saturday 24 April or Saturday 8 May 2027, CHF 300, all equipment provided, credited if you then book) is a separate offer, not part of the course; it runs from 3 participants.
3
April: 4 online theory evenings
19:00 to 21:00, live online in English and recorded for anyone who misses one: 12, 15, 19 and 22 April 2027. Evening 1 meteorology, evening 2 aerodynamics and materials, evening 3 flight technique and airspace law, evening 4 exam questions.
4
Ground school, Thursday 27 to Sunday 30 May
Four days on the training hill in Stans: launching, steering, landing, until the wing stops being a stranger. Thursday is Corpus Christi, a public holiday in Lucerne, so most people only need the Friday off. Video analysis right on the training hill: you see your launch in slow motion before you do the next one. The first high flights follow on the first June weekend, for everyone who is ready.
5
31 May to 15 August: high flights
Intensive weeks from 31 May to 20 June: we fly every day, with one day off a week, usually the one with the worst weather. Then Friday-to-Sunday weekends until 15 August. The weekends are included until your exam or 80 high flights, whichever comes first; a weekend day runs when at least 3 students sign up. Intensive students come every day of the three weeks and top up on the weekends; weekend students come Friday to Sunday, with Saturday and Sunday as the core. The call for each day comes at 20:00 the evening before. Emmetten, Wolfenschiessen, Engelberg and more. Maneuver training, thermal basics, exam preparation. Most students need 55 to 80 high flights before the exam; the course is planned for 60+.
6
Exam-ready
We prepare you for the SHV theory and practical exams; you book the exams with the SHV yourself. Targets: the theory exam by the end of May, the practical exam between the end of June and mid-July. Once the theory is passed you can sign up for the practical straight away, and withdraw up to the day before.
7
Not exam-ready on 15 August?
The course ends on its last day, and there is no extension fee. If you still need flights, you finish at a partner school we trust, with your training record handed over. You pay them directly: typically CHF 20 to 30 per flight, or CHF 150 to 190 per day. Students who need it usually need 10 to 20 flights, about CHF 200 to 500.

This course is for you if…

  • you can take three weeks off in June, or keep your summer weekends for flying, and want the licence this year, not "someday"
  • you want structure, momentum and a fixed group instead of booking single days
  • you value small groups: max. 10 students. A launch assistant is always there in the first weeks and whenever 7 or more students fly, so everyone still gets their flights. Compared with a group of 20, that is twice the instructor time for you
  • you want to learn in English (German and Hungarian work too, including the radio in the air)
  • you are reasonably fit (if you can hike up a small hill, you are fine) and at least 15 years old

This course is NOT for you if…

  • you want to book single days whenever it suits you. The course runs on fixed days for the whole group, and days you miss are not replaced
  • you want the cheapest licence in Switzerland. There are cheaper courses; they run with much bigger groups
  • you expect us to chase the weather to Italy. We stay home, and three intensive weeks plus eight weekends leave room for what the weather takes
  • you want a big club with a big shop and a different instructor every day

Good Questions

What happens if the weather is bad?
Swiss reality: some days will not be flyable. That is why the course runs across a whole summer: a rainy weekend is one of eight, not a third of your course. We decide at 20:00 the evening before. We will not drive you 300 km to Italy to chase the weather. With three intensive weeks and eight weekends, a few lost days do not decide your course.

Is one summer enough to fly safely?
With 55 to 80 high flights you are exam-ready, but a confident pilot keeps flying afterwards. That is the honest answer. We would rather build pilots than print licences.

Can I do the Swiss paragliding licence in one summer in English?
Yes. Briefings, theory evenings and radio guidance run in English; German and Hungarian are available too, including the radio in the air.

What if I am not exam-ready by 15 August?
The course is planned for 60+ high flights, and most students are exam-ready by then. If you still need flights, you finish at a partner school we trust, with your training record handed over: typically CHF 20 to 30 per flight or CHF 150 to 190 per day, usually about CHF 200 to 500 in total, paid directly to the partner school. There is no extension fee on this course.

What happens after the course?
We hand you over to the local clubs and, if you want more coached flights, to partner schools we trust. For licensed pilots we run the FLYwithMIKI Academy: coaching days, 1:1 mentoring and advanced theory, March to August.

Do I need my own equipment?
Yes, from the first high flight. Either our beginner set, chosen and sized for you and yours to keep, or your own kit after our airworthiness review. With your own kit the course is CHF 6,000: our course price is low because it is partly funded by the equipment package, and we prefer to tell you that openly. That way we earn about the same whichever you choose, so our advice on your gear can stay honest. School gear is available only on the spring Trial Days.

I started training somewhere else. Can I join?
Yes. After a short level-assessment call you join the course where it fits you. If you live in Switzerland you need the SHV licence to fly here regularly; a foreign licence or an IPPI card covers visiting pilots, not residents.

Can I book single days instead?
No. The course runs on fixed days for the whole group: that is how one instructor can teach a small group well without being on standby all year. Days you miss yourself are not replaced. If the summer does not work for you, join the list for 2028, or ask us for a partner school.

When do you answer messages?
During the course the plan for the next day comes at 20:00 in the group. Messages get an answer within one working day, usually within a few hours. Longer calls take place in the office hours, Tuesday and Thursday, 17:00 to 19:00.

Deposit, go / no-go and cancellation?
A deposit of CHF 1,500 secures your place; a second CHF 1,500 is due by 31 March 2027 (if you book later, both at once) and the rest on the first day. The deposit is not refundable, but it can be transferred once, to a named person who takes your place. The course runs from 4 paid deposits; we decide on 31 March 2027. With fewer than 4 deposits by then, you choose: everything you paid back in full, or a smaller course with a CHF 1,500 surcharge per person. Days you miss yourself are used up. The beginner set is paid when we order it for you, by 15 April 2027 at the latest. Instalments are possible, we agree them with you personally.
