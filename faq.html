Let's start with the official answer, not with a scare: SUVA does not classify paragliding as a high-risk sport, and your standard Swiss accident insurance covers it, no surcharge, no special policy. Very few adventure sports can say that.

That said, it is aviation, and it carries real risks; anyone who tells you otherwise is selling, not teaching. What the statistics and 30+ years of Swiss training experience show is that risk is overwhelmingly a function of training quality and pilot judgment: not luck. That's why you fly with a radio in your ear on every single flight, why there are never more than 10 students in the course, why honest weather decisions are part of the curriculum ("YOU are the pilot, YOU decide", but never alone while you learn), and why mental training is built into the course.
Very likely yes, and you're not alone, this is one of the most common questions we get. Fear of heights and fear of flying are two different things: height-anxiety is triggered by standing at an edge with your body telling you that you could fall. Under a wing you are sitting securely in a harness, moving with the air, most height-anxious people report feeling surprisingly calm. We start on gentle training hills a few meters above the slope and progress only at your pace. A healthy respect for the air is not a weakness; it's the foundation of a good pilot.
